## STAR Story 3: Critical Thinking And Decision Making

Question:
Tell me about a time you had ambiguity and had to make a technical decision.

Answer:
> In S/4HANA programs, one recurring ambiguity is deciding between brownfield and greenfield migration approaches, and also deciding what should remain in core SAP versus what should be extended through SAP BTP. There is rarely a perfect answer because each option has cost, timeline, risk, and long-term maintainability implications.
>
> My approach was to first clarify the business priorities: speed of migration, process redesign appetite, regulatory or compliance constraints, integration complexity, and long-term operating model. Then I assessed the technical landscape, custom code, upgrade impact, data dependencies, and integration risks.
>
> Based on that analysis, I recommended a path that balanced business continuity with modernization. I also defined Clean Core standards so the decision did not only solve the immediate project but reduced future upgrade complexity. This helped stakeholders make a reasoned decision instead of treating migration as a purely technical debate.

Use Good Judgement, Innovate & Improve, Be an Owner.

## Strong Phrases To Use

- "I tried to connect the technical decision to business impact."
- "I made the tradeoff visible to stakeholders."
- "I aligned the team on decision criteria before jumping to a solution."
- "I focused on preventing defects upstream, not just resolving them downstream."
- "I treated the issue as an ownership problem, not only a technical problem."
- "I used data, risk, and long-term maintainability to guide the decision."
- "I made sure we disagreed, aligned, and committed."

## Avoid Saying

- "I only handled the technical part."
- "The offshore team caused the issue."
- "The business did not give clear requirements."
- "I worked very hard" without explaining outcome.
- "I know everything in SAP."
- "I prefer only architecture, not delivery."
